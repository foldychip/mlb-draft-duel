ℹ️ How are player ratings decided?
Every player gets a rating from 45 to 99 built from real career performance, not guesswork. The main ingredients:
  • How good they were per game — hitters are judged on era-adjusted offense (OPS+), pitchers on run prevention (ERA+). This asks "how dominant when they played?"
  • Total career value — a career-long measure (WAR) that rewards sustained greatness, defense, and longevity. This carries the most weight, so a long great career outranks a short hot streak.
  • Awards & honors — MVP and Cy Young wins give a big boost, with smaller credit for 2nd/3rd-place finishes, plus All-Star selections, Silver Sluggers, and Gold Gloves.
On top of that, a few fairness adjustments fix things a single stat misses:
  • Speed & defense — base-stealers and elite glovemen get credit the batting stats ignore.
  • Elite closers — relief aces get a boost, since career totals undersell short-inning relievers.
  • Young stars — players early in their careers are judged on their pace, so a dominant rookie isn't punished for a short résumé.
  • Great bats, short careers — a feared slugger whose career was cut short still gets credit for the bat.
  • Hall of Famers — any inducted Hall of Famer is rated at least 97.
Numbers are approximate, for a fun game — not official. Well-known players use real stats; a few deep-bench players are reasonable estimates.
